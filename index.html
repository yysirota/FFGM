Scouting Lab

Independent expert opinions, disagreements and upside/downside research. Not crowd values.
How six experts become one number

Week 10, 2026 · six current experts: FF Dataroma, Elisha Twerski, Draft Sharks, Justin Boone, Kev Wheeler, and Nick Mariano. All six receive equal weight (16⅔% each for a fully covered player). Dataroma is already a 0–100 trade price, Twerski Half-PPR prices are normalized by 78, Draft Sharks 3D values use their published trade scale, and Boone Half-PPR prices are normalized by 101. Boone's 2QB prices use 105 as the maximum in Superflex, and his 1QB prices are used in one-QB leagues. The other three priced sources retain the Week 10 QB rank-to-Superflex conversion. Wheeler's and Mariano's newly supplied Half-PPR overall rankings are ordinal, not trade dollars; to avoid inventing expert prices, each RB/WR/TE's position in their skill-player order maps to the fixed Week 10 four-expert market value curve. Their QB positional ordering maps to the corresponding Superflex or one-QB QB curve. Those are transparent calibration rules—not prices published by Wheeler/Mariano. Missing opinions are never treated as zero. Coverage adjustments range from 1.5% for five sources to 18% for one. Tight ends without a mandatory TE starting slot retain the Draft Sharks ROS WR-equivalent adjustment. These six opinions are not independent performance forecasts; rank-to-price conversion is an approximation. No previous-week sources are carried forward.

Scenario Lab

Stress-test your own roster against injury, breakouts or regression. These are hypothetical scenarios—not forecasts or win probabilities.
How scenario analysis works

FFGM reoptimizes the team's actual starting slots after removing the selected player or applying the selected hypothetical change in model value. Existing bench players can move into the lineup. It does not estimate the likelihood of injuries, breakout events, weekly game results, or a statistically validated championship probability.

Private ESPN league? Use secure local export No passwords or cookies shared

An ESPN league ID alone cannot open a private league. A league member can download their own roster data while signed in to ESPN, then import that JSON here. The exporter runs inside ESPN, contacts ESPN only, and saves the data locally.

  1. On a computer, sign in to your private league at fantasy.espn.com.
  2. In Fantasy Football GM, enter the league ID and season above, then click Copy export script.
  3. On the ESPN league page, open the browser developer Console, inspect the script before pasting, then run it. It downloads a league JSON file.
  4. Return here and choose Import ESPN JSON below.
